Why pruning could help—without proving it did

The official metric is a distance-weighted Tversky index using a 300 m triangular kernel, α=0.2 and β=0.8. Its false-negative weight is larger than its false-positive weight. A sparser, correlated ridge could retain useful proximity credit while reducing excess prediction mass and therefore false-positive cost. That is a plausible mechanism consistent with the owner-reported 0.2600 D2.8 and 0.2778 B2 claims.

But the pixel lineage alone does not show where hidden truth lies, how much true-positive mass or false-positive mass changed, whether the claimed scores belong to those exact bytes, or whether the pruning caused the score difference. The private labels and per-file organizer receipts are unavailable. Neither B2's claimed value nor this mechanism proves global optimality.

Masking caveat: organizer staff have said exact known USGS/INGENIOUS fault pixels are excluded; no two-pixel scoring buffer is confirmed. Points near mapped faults are therefore not categorically guaranteed to be false positives or to lose all proximity credit: nearby withheld truth may matter.

The literal registry stop

A registered binary lattice covers 5,160,724 of 5,167,373 footprint cells within three pixels. For a 37,654-dot map, at least 82.34% must overlap it—above the literal 70% stop. This bound does not rule out every smaller budget. Separately, a registered continuous probability prior is positive throughout the footprint; if the shared finite-positive support rule counts that continuous field as “dots,” every nonempty candidate has 100% overlap. That interpretation is unresolved and cannot be silently waived.

Result: no surface was placed, no final-dot comparison was run, and no TIFF was created. The project fails closed rather than calling an unsafe raster unique.
