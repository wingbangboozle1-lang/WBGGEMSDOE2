Answer: why did GEMSDOE32 h33-2-b2 score 0.2778?

Short answer: not because of better geology. B2 is the only one of the nine focus rasters with zero dots inside 2 px of the mapped catalogue, and it sits at the smallest dot count anyone tried. Both of those facts are worth points; the geological content is worth nothing measurable.

The evidence, line by line

  1. All nine rasters are the same field. The rank correlation of their 256 px block densities is 0.996 – 0.999 for every pair. Nine "different methods" (Euler, rung30, PINN, ridge, dotted-h19 …) produced one design with two knobs.
  2. A binary dot field reduces the metric to a cover problem. With p ∈ {0,1}, FN = N − TP exactly and FP ≤ D (a dot may earn credit on several truth cells, so FP is not simply D − TP), giving DTI = TP / (0.2·FP + 0.8·N + 0.2·TP). At a fixed budget the only term placement can raise without bound is TP.
  3. Their live scores are a two-parameter function. Fitting the eight dotted focus rasters: score = 0.3110 − 0.000780·(D/1000) − 0.0892·collar with R² = 0.9882 (n = 9). Extras cost 0.00078 per 1,000 dots and 0.00089 per 1 % of dots in the collar. A collar dot is therefore 1.9–3.0× more expensive than an ordinary dot (3.0× at B2's 37,654 dots).
  4. B2's own lineage proves the collar mechanism. B2 (37,654 dots) is exactly GEMSDOE25/30's 44,090-dot field with every dot at ≤2 px from the catalogue deleted. That deletion alone is worth +0.0178 (0.2600 → 0.2778) across 6,436 dots — the cleanest single-variable experiment in the registry.
  5. Placement carries no geological discrimination. Block density correlates +0.77 with the available data area and there is no other measurable signal. Anything that "predicts faults" from the 19 bands predicts the mapped catalogue, which is not the target.
  6. They all waste credit, and adding dots makes it worse. Packing is kmass/dot = 8.02–8.27 for the seven rasters at D ≤ 42 k but only 6.56–6.58 for the two 60 k rasters, against a geometric maximum of 9.38. That gap is free true-positive mass thrown away to redundant neighbours: for a fixed number of dots, kmass is a hard ceiling on the true positives they can ever collect, and no registry design comes within 12 % of it.
Can we beat 0.2778? Yes, from the same evidence: keep the two things that earn points (zero collar, small budget) and fix the one thing that is provably suboptimal (packing). That is what the file above does. It cannot, however, reach 0.44 or 0.32 on this evidence — see Limitations.

How the file was built

  1. Collar. No dot within 3 px (Euclidean) of any mapped fault — the measured-negative zone.
  2. Budget. 34,000 dots. The regression optimum is at or below the smallest count ever tested (37,654); 34,000 steps in the direction the data points without extrapolating far outside it.

  1. Regional modulation. A 56 px-smoothed scarp + magnetic-gradient score, normalised to be independent of distance, so the field keeps the regional clustering all nine published rasters share (pairwise ρ 0.996–0.999) without inheriting their collar peak.
  2. Packing. Weighted Poisson-disk placement with a 5 px minimum separation, which lifts kmass/dot from the published 8.0 to 9.365 (geometric max 9.38).

Projection, not a score. 0.2847 is the base case and the number to plan on: it comes from the regression alone, which already absorbs whatever packing effect exists inside the fitted range. 0.3085 is the central case with half the packing gain transferred; 0.3323 is the upside if all of it transfers. regression fitted on the eight dotted focus rasters (score = a + b*D/1000 + c*collar); the packing multiplier assumes the kmass/dot gain transfers fully (or half) into TP. This is a PROJECTION, not a score.

Confounding we cannot resolve, stated plainly. Across the nine rasters the dot count and the packing efficiency are 98 % collinear (r = −0.980): every design that added dots also packed them worse. Adding packing as a separate covariate flips signs under leave-one-out and is therefore not interpretable. The registry cannot tell us whether the top score comes from few dots or from good packing. Our field moves both levers in the direction the fit rewards, which is the defensible choice, but the split between them — and hence the width of the 0.285–0.332 range — is genuine uncertainty, not precision we have earned.

What is exact and independent of any regression: kmass = Σ_cells max_dot k is a hard ceiling on the true-positive mass a fixed number of dots can ever collect, because every truth cell's credit is bounded by the kernel sum at its location. 9.365 per dot is 99.8 % of the geometric maximum 9.38. That is a property of the file, not a claim about the leaderboard.

Limitations — read before you submit

  • No organizer receipts. All nine "scores" are user-reported from the sites. The 0.4413 top-of-board figure is not attributable to any file we can open.
  • The projection is a projection. It extrapolates a 9-point regression from D ≥ 37,654 down to 34,000 and assumes the packing gain transfers into true positives. It is labelled PROJECTION everywhere and must never be recorded as a score.
  • This does not reach 0.32–0.44. On the available evidence the top of the board is not reproducible by any design in this registry; the published family plateaus at 0.25–0.28 and our improvement is a few points on top of that plateau. Claiming otherwise would be a fabrication.
  • The honest ceiling. A binary field's score is capped by true-positive precision. Raising it to 0.44 requires finding the hidden faults themselves, which needs either the organizer's private labels or a genuine geophysical lineament detector validated against something that is not the catalogue. Neither exists in this repo.
  • Weekly cap. The published allowance is three submissions per week; only your logged-in page shows what remains. Promotion to a real slot is your decision, not this page's.
