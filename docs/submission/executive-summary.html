2. Why did GEMSDOE32’s file score highest of the nine (0.2778)?

Three things are measurable; one thing is not.

  1. It is the least redundant dot cloud in the winning family. Reported scores fall monotonically as emitted mass grows: 37,654 dots → 0.2778 · 37,660 → 0.2710 · 40,199 → 0.2708 · 41,865 → 0.2632 · 44,090 → 0.2600 · 60,069 → 0.2477 · 61,328 → 0.2449. Deriving the metric's marginal economics (scripts/mass_balance.py) explains why: because FN_w = |G| − TP_w exactly, the official DTI collapses to T/(T + 0.2F + 0.8(N−T)), so a unit of mass that hits truth is worth ≈ +0.94 while a unit that misses costs only ≈ −0.056… and redundant mass that duplicates an existing hit earns nothing but still costs. Pruning therefore pays, and the file with the fewest dots inside the same prior won.
  2. All nine files are one lineage, not nine ideas. Measured mutual overlap is 83–100% at the 3-px tolerance, and 24 ↔ 25 ↔ 30 ↔ 32 are effectively identical (≈1.00). 0.2778 is not evidence that its method is better; it is the same surface, pruned and re-emitted.
  3. It sits off the catalogue, not on it. Median offset 18 px (1.8 km) from mapped traces and only 7% of dots within 3 px of one. The decisive control is the fleet's own gems79-tilt-ring-100000dots: it lies on the catalogue (1.00 of dots within 3 px) and scored 0.0005 (user-reported). The competition's labels are expert-identified faults that are absent from the catalogue, so catalogue-derived mass buys almost nothing.

Not measurable here: which geophysical prior the lineage actually used. The GEMSDOE32 page and the fleet's own validators mark their files owner-reported and research-only, and the training_features.tif stack those pipelines used (419 MB, pinned sha256 4371c82e…23bc5) is behind DrivenData authentication. So the honest attribution is: pruning + off-catalogue placement inside a good prior, not a physics result we can reproduce.

3. Can we generate something that scores above 0.2778?

Demonstrated: no. Plausible: yes, marginally; Reliable: not with the data reachable from this environment.

  • The live leaderboard leader is 0.4544 (source, retrieved 2026-10-27). Every fleet file in the requester's list sits 0.17–0.25 below that. A fleet that has spent ~90 sites on catalogue geometry has not closed that gap, which is itself evidence that the gap is not a tuning problem.
  • Our candidate makes the two moves the evidence supports — fewer, non-redundant dots (34,500) at a calibrated off-catalogue offset (1.4 km median) — and its conditional band, if its hit rate matches the fleet's best lineage, is 0.278–0.320. That is the honest way to say “it could edge past 0.2778”: it depends entirely on prior quality, which we cannot measure without the feature stack.
  • The empirical model's point estimate (0.15) is lower than 0.2778 because our prior is a different family from the winning lineage and the model penalises that. Both numbers are published here rather than the flattering one.

4. Candidate hypotheses, ranked (5 named, 1 implemented)

Each names the layers involved, the physical signature, why it should find faults missing from the catalogue rather than ones already in it, and how it differs from what this repository and the fleet have already done. Ranking is by expected DTI gain per unit of implementation cost.

#Hypothesis / layersPhysical signatureWhy off-catalogue faults Non-fault mimicDifference from this repo & the fleetCost · status
1 DZ-16 damage-zone flank. Layers: existing_faults.tif geometry only (the ideal addition, training_features.tif bands 1–2 surface conductivity & depth-to-conductive-base, is not obtainable here). Annulus at 1.6 km ± 1.2 km from mapped traces, weighted by 2 km local fault density; emitted as isolated binary dots ≥ 800 m apart. Regional compilations rasterise long continuous traces and drop sub-parallel strands and relay links; those sit at a characteristic perpendicular offset, so the prior is exactly zero on the mapped trace. Lithological contact parallel to a fault zone; a single large fault's damage zone mistaken for a splay. The fleet's dotted/topo-gap/tip/stepover families all emit on or along traces or between tips; none uses a calibrated perpendicular annulus with a hard trace exclusion and a separation floor. Low · implemented (this submission)
2 Magnetic-source-depth vs dilatation-rate cross-term. Layers: top-of-crustal magnetic source depth (band ~9) × dilatation rate (band 4). Coincident shallow magnetic basement and high dilatation rate; a cross product suppresses either signal alone. A concealed basement fault offsets the source-depth surface and localises strain, while the published trace layer cannot see through cover. Intrusive body or alteration clay cap changing apparent source depth. Bands exist in the official stack but no fleet run pairs source depth with strain rate as an interaction; the fleet's magnetic work is gradient/intensity based. High · blocked (feature stack unavailable)
3 Second-invariant strain ridge × gravity-gradient trough. Layers: second invariant of the strain rate tensor (band 6) × slope of isostatic gravity anomaly (band 8). Linear crest of the strain second invariant coinciding with a gravity gradient trough edge. Active strain localisation is independent of whether a fault has been mapped; density contrast adds subsurface continuity. Basin-margin density gradient unrelated to faulting; smoothing artefacts at survey seams. The fleet tried strain–seismicity and gravity–magnetic pairs; this specific second-invariant × gradient-crossing product is not in the lineage. Medium-high · blocked (same data)
4 Seismicity anisotropy conditioned on structural strike. Layer: earthquake density (band 14) conditioned on local fault strike. Event clouds elongated parallel to an independently derived structural strike, not merely dense. Unmapped active structures produce aligned microseismicity that density alone cannot separate from aftershock clusters. Aftershock sequences, swarms, quarry blasts, and catalogue completeness edges. Fleet runs used raw seismicity density; conditioning on strike alignment is untried and the layer is coarse, so the prior must stay low-weight. Medium · blocked (same data)
5 1 m lidar scarp asymmetry at the 300 m kernel scale. Source: the official 1m_DEM_links.csv on the competition data page. Down-slope-facing scarp asymmetry and channel deflection measured at 3-px kernel scale. Fault scarps are visible in high-resolution relief long before they are mapped, especially in unconsolidated basin fill. Landslide head scarps, roads, ditches, terrace edges, and DEM vegetation artefacts. Fleet scarp files use 10 m DEM derivatives; 1 m lidar at the metric's own support radius is untried. High · blocked — the link CSV is behind DrivenData login (verified redirect; see Sources)

Only hypothesis 1 could be built and validated with data reachable from this environment. Hypotheses 2–5 all require training_features.tif (or the 1 m DEM link list), which is behind authentication: the environment's network reaches github.com, codeload.github.com, api.github.com, registry.npmjs.org, pypi.org and files.pythonhosted.org only — drivendata.org, usgs.gov and dropbox.com are unreachable. Naming the source honestly is not the same as having it.

6. Limitations you should assume are real

  • No true validation. The competition's labels are expert-identified faults absent from the public catalogue, and they are not obtainable here. Everything called “validation” on this site is a proxy, a model estimate, or a conditional band — never a holdout DTI against organizer labels.
  • The uniqueness sweep measured 96 of the 655 grid rasters in the fleet index (the index itself only covers GEMSDOE…GEMSDOE47). Newer rasters were not measured.
  • The empirical model has negative out-of-sample R². It is a calibration aid with a wide interval, not a predictive instrument.
  • Geological confirmation is not automated. The technical gates are automated and reproducible; whether the dots correspond to real faults is a question for the competition's expert panel.
