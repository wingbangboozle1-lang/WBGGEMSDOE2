P1 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 1 · MEDIUM–HIGH COST

Upward-continuation ridge trajectories in magnetic data

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Highest qualitative potential among these desk proposals; untested here; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: rtp magnetic anomaly (owner-mirror band 2); tmi_vg (band 9), context only; det_elev_slope (band 19), terrain sensitivity

Physical signature: Compute masked, edge-controlled upward continuations of the magnetic field at several declared heights, track the displacement and persistence of oriented ridge maxima across heights, and test a coherent trajectory rather than selecting one amplitude edge. Exclude or explicitly model FFT boundary and survey-seam effects.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A buried basement step can remain detectable in potential-field data where the surface fault map is incomplete. Variation across continuation heights may help distinguish broad source geometry, but does not prove a fault or its position.

Difference from reviewed work: The local MSPTCD code uses multi-scale gradients and magnetic tilt, but not ridge trajectories across a separately computed continuation series. GEMSDOE32 already lists TMI_up150 in related methods, so this is a proposed extension of an existing family, not a claim of fleet-wide originality. The earlier phase-congruency idea is withdrawn because GEMSDOE32 reports an ARENA49-PCG implementation.

Named non-fault mimic: Lithologic contacts, intrusive dikes, remanent magnetization, uneven survey height, leveling seams, and FFT edge artifacts.

Data/access status: Uses owner-mirrored magnetic and terrain bands, not authenticated organizer inputs. Continuation requires defensible units, nodata and boundary handling; no operator, canary, holdout or registry preflight was run.

P2 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 2 · MEDIUM–HIGH COST

Matched cross-flank texture displacement

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Moderate qualitative potential; untested here; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: rtp magnetic anomaly (band 2); det_elev (band 12); det_elev_slope (band 19)

Physical signature: For independently detected candidate lines, search bounded two-dimensional translations between matched oriented texture windows on opposite flanks and require consistent displacement estimates across multiple window sizes; estimate nuisance statistics only on training regions.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A degraded or buried fault can offset a pre-existing magnetic or terrain pattern without producing a continuous mapped scarp; matched texture could suggest the missing line, but it cannot establish causation.

Difference from reviewed work: The local implementation uses pointwise orientation and Hessian ridges rather than matched-window translation estimates. GEMSDOE28 lists a related two-sided texture-covariance idea as unimplemented and GEMSDOE24 lists a related texture-front proposal; this variant must not be described as wholly new to the fleet.

Named non-fault mimic: Intrusive boundaries, unconformities, erosional contacts, irregular textures and gridding seams can give apparent offsets.

Data/access status: Owner-mirrored bands were restored and hash-checked, not authenticated. Window quality, missing data, displacement ambiguity and fold-local controls have not been tested.

P3 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 3 · HIGH COST

Focal-mechanism nodal-plane consistency

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Lower qualitative potential; external coverage unverified; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS ComCat focal-mechanism or moment-tensor products; local magnetic and terrain structural orientations, if independently derived

Physical signature: For adequately located earthquakes, propagate both ambiguous nodal planes and hypocentral uncertainty into a broad spatial orientation likelihood, then test agreement with independently measured structural orientations; never treat epicenters or nodal planes as exact fault traces.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: Seismic ruptures can reflect concealed structures lacking a surface trace. Events with resolved mechanisms might provide orientation evidence where topography is subdued, if coverage and temporal representativeness are adequate.

Difference from reviewed work: The inspected local implementation uses seismicity density and strain proxies, not explicit uncertainty-weighted focal nodal planes. GEMSDOE32 previously proposed a ComCat-related idea but reported it data-blocked, so the source and broader family are not new.

Named non-fault mimic: Induced seismicity, unrelated deeper faults, uncertain locations, catalog completeness changes, and the two-plane ambiguity.

Data/access status: Not viable here until specific event-product coverage, timestamps, quality and binary/API access are verified; no ComCat records were retrieved in this sandbox.

External source/access: USGS ComCat FDSN event service ↗. Official documentation describes a public event API, producttype filters for focal-mechanism and moment-tensor products, and event-specific associated products. This sandbox has not queried the target region or verified usable mechanism counts, access or competition sharing.

P4 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 4 · HIGH COST

Groundwater-head discontinuity across aquifers

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Exploratory; external coverage unverified; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS Water Data for the Nation groundwater-level and monitoring-location records; aquifer identity, observation time, reference elevation and uncertainty metadata; independent magnetic or terrain candidate orientations

Physical signature: Compare coeval hydraulic heads from comparable aquifer depths on opposite sides of a candidate line, with uncertainty-aware discontinuity against a smooth regional groundwater gradient; require enough well pairs and do not interpolate sparse gaps into certain faults.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: Some faults restrict groundwater flow and produce head offsets even where alluvium hides surface expressions. The effect varies by aquifer and not all head discontinuities are faults.

Difference from reviewed work: The inspected local implementation uses no matched hydraulic-head discontinuity. Historical heat-flow, springs and thermal/geochemical proximity experiments are related but measure different variables; unreviewed sibling implementations may still exist.

Named non-fault mimic: Pumping or recharge, irrigation, aquifer and depth changes, time offsets, stratigraphic barriers and datum errors.

Data/access status: Not viable until target-region well pairing, aquifer metadata, dates and machine-accessible bytes are checked. No data were downloaded here; modern API rate limits and possible free-key requirements must be checked for the eventual workflow.

External source/access: USGS Water Data for the Nation modern APIs ↗. Official USGS pages describe public groundwater and metadata APIs; modern services may require a free API key for more than a handful of requests per hour. Target-area coverage, water-level records and sandbox access were not verified.
