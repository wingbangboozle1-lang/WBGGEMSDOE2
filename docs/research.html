H1 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 1 · MEDIUM COST

Depth-shifted, multiscale gravity–magnetic discontinuity

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Highest qualitative potential of this shortlist; untested; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: iso_grav_anom (owner-mirror band 13); rtp (owner-mirror band 2); depth_to_base_surf (owner-mirror band 15; official metadata says conductive-base depth, not verified basement depth); det_elev (owner-mirror band 12)

Physical signature: At several fixed spatial scales, compare signed gravity-edge normals with magnetic-gradient ridges at a bounded cross-normal offset informed by a depth/dip proxy; test both magnetic polarities and retain only scale-persistent paired edges.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A buried structural discontinuity may juxtapose density and magnetic properties beneath basin fill where a surface trace is weak or absent. The proposal uses subsurface physical-property contrasts rather than catalogue proximity. This is a plausible target for unmapped segments, not proof that the catalogue omits only surface-visible faults.

Difference from reviewed work: Different as proposed from the named pseudo-gravity residual, simple potential-field fusion, dotted-ridge, catalogue-gap and geothermal-PINN families: it tests a depth-conditioned spatial offset between paired signed edge responses. Related methods elsewhere are not exhaustively audited.

Named non-fault mimic: An unfaulted lithologic contact or intrusive dike can produce aligned gravity and magnetic edges; flight-line striping and gridding artefacts can also create apparent paired boundaries.

Data/access status: The 19-band feature names are recorded in a hash-checked owner mirror, not an authenticated DrivenData download. The local data/cache and derived shared feature bank are absent in this checkout; the pinned shared template must be restored and provenance rechecked before any run.

H2 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 2 · MEDIUM COST

Conductive-flank asymmetry conditioned on a potential-field edge

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Moderate qualitative potential; overlaps a previously explored family; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: cond_surf (owner-mirror band 17); depth_to_base_surf (owner-mirror band 15; official metadata terminology must be retained); iso_grav_anom_hg (owner-mirror band 18) and iso_grav_anom_vg (owner-mirror band 11); det_elev_slope (owner-mirror band 19) as topographic context

Physical signature: Estimate signed conductivity contrast on opposite sides of a persistent gravity edge, normalize each flank against its local background and depth, and test whether the asymmetry is stable across scales rather than using raw conductivity or a symmetric buffer.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A concealed fault-related fluid or alteration pathway could make the two flanks electrically asymmetric even where a mapped trace is missing. It is a conditional physical-property test, not a rule that high conductivity means a fault.

Difference from reviewed work: The proposed statistic is a signed, edge-conditioned cross-flank contrast rather than simple co-location or a vent-proximity prior. However, the user-provided history includes conductivity/flank-labelled methods, so this is not claimed to be globally novel and should rank below H1 unless exact old code shows a meaningful distinction.

Named non-fault mimic: Saline basin fill, clay-rich strata, groundwater, and non-faulted lithologic boundaries can all create conductivity asymmetry.

Data/access status: Uses bands named in the owner-mirrored numerical feature stack; the original competition bytes and current local derived cache are not available/authenticated here.

H3 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 3 · HIGH COST

Repeated cross-drainage deflection across a candidate structure

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Lower qualitative potential; external-data feasibility unverified; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS 3DEP bare-earth digital elevation model (specific tiles and resolution not established here); det_elev_slope (owner-mirror band 19) for comparison only

Physical signature: Derive channels from absolute elevation, measure repeated channel azimuth changes, knickzones, and opposing-bank relief across the same candidate line, and normalize by upstream contributing area; require several independent crossings rather than a single scarp.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A buried or eroded fault may deflect multiple channels without leaving a continuous scarp, creating a geomorphic expression beyond catalogue segment endpoints. Channel behaviour is supporting evidence, not a fault label.

Difference from reviewed work: Different from single-scarp, slope-asymmetry, chi-corridor and dotted-ridge methods because it tests repeated cross-channel geometry using an absolute DEM and catchment normalization. Fleet-wide novelty is unverified.

Named non-fault mimic: Lithologic contacts, differential erosion, alluvial fans, inherited drainage, capture, roads, and terraces can deflect channels.

Data/access status: Not viable in this checkout until the exact official DEM tiles, coverage, resolution and permitted download are verified; none were downloaded.

External source/access: USGS 3DEP products and services ↗. The cited USGS page is recorded in the source register as describing free, unrestricted 3DEP products. Study-area tile coverage and actual binary access have not been checked; therefore the data are not yet verified obtainable for this experiment.

H4 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 4 · HIGH COST

Cross-flight-line repeatability of buried magnetic edges

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Lowest direct prediction potential; primarily an artefact-control experiment; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS GeoDAWN magnetic flight-line observations and acquisition-direction metadata; mag_anom (owner-mirror band 1) and tmi_hg (owner-mirror band 3) for gridded comparison

Physical signature: Estimate edge normals independently from crossing flight-line directions and retain only anomalies whose location and orientation are reproducible across acquisition directions; use disagreement as an artefact veto, not as a positive fault label.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A real subsurface physical-property boundary potentially associated with an unmapped fault should be spatially stable across independent acquisition directions, unlike some flight-line artefacts. This test improves measurement reliability; it does not establish that a stable edge is a fault.

Difference from reviewed work: This is an acquisition-geometry robustness test, not another point-density, terrain-scarp, geothermal-proximity, or catalogue-gap score. No local implementation is present; fleet-wide novelty is unverified.

Named non-fault mimic: A lithologic contact, dike, or non-fault magnetic body is also stable across flight directions; uneven line coverage can create false disagreement.

Data/access status: The USGS release page is recorded as listing flight-line data and grids, but the line-level files and usable crossing coverage were not downloaded or verified in this environment. Not viable here until that check succeeds.

External source/access: USGS GeoDAWN airborne magnetic and radiometric survey release ↗. The public release page is recorded as available and CC0 in the source register; its binary source host was not reachable in the prior sandbox check. Exact flight-line file access and spatial overlap remain unverified.
