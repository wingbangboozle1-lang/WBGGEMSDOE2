Preliminary ranking · qualitative only

Four physically grounded hypotheses.

Expected value is a research judgment, not a score. Novelty is only relative to inspected work; it is not an exhaustive literature or concurrent-lane search.

01

Radiometric contact shear offset · medium cost

Compare along-strike shifts of potassium/thorium contacts on either side of coherent LiDAR scarps. An unmapped fault could displace a contact; terraces, roads, stratigraphic pinch-outs and survey artefacts can mimic it. This differs from simply matching parallel magnetic and topographic gradients.

02

Terrain / stratigraphy discordance · low–medium cost

Test whether a coherent scarp cuts across a radiometric unit boundary. Streams, mines and missing data can also produce cross-cutting patterns.

03

Conductive-basement step · medium cost

Combine a lateral conductive-base depth change with contrasting radiometry. Buried facies boundaries and inversion-resolution artefacts remain alternatives.

04

Paleothermal lineament intersections · high cost

Test GDR sinter/tufa locations against intersecting persistent LiDAR lineaments with matched sampling-effort controls. Depositional and survey bias are potential mimics; spatial coverage has not been independently checked.

Verified input caveat

Trace the data, not just the hash.

The official USGS GeoDAWN release describes airborne magnetic and radiometric data with coordinated lidar; it is marked CC0. The GDR INGENIOUS record includes paleogeothermal, fault, temperature and other layers under CC BY 4.0. The aligned layers and template used in the audit are owner-hosted mirrors: their hashes are pinned, but their organizer origin was not independently authenticated.

Protocol notes

Open issues, not hidden assumptions.

  • The mirror sample's positive support equals the fault-label support, whereas the official description calls the sample fault-absence; only its grid and finite footprint were used.
  • Two GEMSDOE32 command-line wrappers binarize soft predictions. We use the pinned shared GEMSDOE57 evaluator/writer for fixtures and report the upstream issue; no private fork was made.
  • The official public leaderboard and historical user reports are different evidence classes. Scores from different holdout protocols are not directly comparable.
  • A successful local format check cannot guarantee portal acceptance or a high private score.
