N1 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 1 · MEDIUM COST

Signed edge-pair cancellation as a non-fault-body veto

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Highest qualitative potential in this revised shortlist; no measured improvement; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: rtp (owner-mirror band 2); iso_grav_anom (owner-mirror band 13); depth_to_base_surf (owner-mirror band 15; conductive-base depth proxy only)

Physical signature: Along gravity-edge normals, sample signed cross-normal gradient profiles at fixed 200, 400 and 800 m smoothing scales. Distinguish a persistent monotonic step from a balanced pair of opposite-polarity lobes returning to background; penalize the latter as a finite-width body hypothesis. Require consistency along strike, not just a high local gradient.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A buried density step can persist under basin fill without a mapped surface trace. This hypothesis tries to reject finite-width magnetic bodies rather than reward proximity to the public catalogue. Some real fault zones also produce paired lobes, so the veto must earn its value on buffered hide-and-recover folds.

Difference from reviewed work: The local MSPTCD implementation uses unsigned shifted gradient amplitudes and local orientation products, not signed profile return-to-background or paired-lobe cancellation. This is a proposed discriminant, not the already-tested depth-shifted-concordance H1. Prior fleet-wide use cannot be excluded.

Named non-fault mimic: A one-sided unfaulted intrusive or lithologic contact can still resemble a structural step; a real horst or narrow fault-bounded block can resemble a finite-width body and be incorrectly vetoed.

N2 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 2 · MEDIUM COST

Radiometric-quiet magnetic-edge transfer under conductive cover

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Moderate qualitative potential; targets a mapping-bias regime rather than more dots; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: tc (owner-mirror band 6; radiometric interpretation plausible, units require source metadata); rtp (owner-mirror band 2); depth_to_base_surf (owner-mirror band 15); cond_surf (owner-mirror band 17; fold-local rank strata, units unverified); det_elev_slope (owner-mirror band 19)

Physical signature: Find magnetic ridges that persist across 300–1000 m smoothing but lose their co-located surface total-count edge in low-relief, deeper-conductive-base strata. Match sampling within fold-local B17 conductivity-rank strata and train any regime threshold on visible-training cells only. Compare this radiometric-quiet branch with the same magnetic-edge model and sampling without the branch.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A subsurface contrast may remain magnetically visible while young sediment conceals a surface lithologic or scarp expression, a plausible omission regime for surface mapping. Radiometric quietness is a cover/measurement hypothesis, not positive evidence of a geothermal vent or a fault by itself.

Difference from reviewed work: MSPTCD treats terrain-normalized total-count edges as an additional positive feature. This proposal instead tests explicit cross-modal edge disappearance as a cover-conditioned transfer rule, with matched sampling and a negative control. The earlier conductivity-flank and depth-offset ideas are already implemented and are not reused as new hypotheses.

Named non-fault mimic: Moist soil, uniform sediment composition, variable aircraft clearance, weak radiometric signal or a buried non-faulted volcanic/plutonic contact can produce the same discordance.

N3 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 3 · HIGH COST

Catalogue-free edge-orientation transport through structural junctions

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Exploratory qualitative potential; high cost and nonunique geological interpretation; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: iso_grav_anom (owner-mirror band 13); rtp (owner-mirror band 2); det_elev (owner-mirror band 12; detrended, not an absolute hydrologic DEM)

Physical signature: Build an edge graph from label-free gravity and magnetic ridges. Transport axial normals along each ridge across scales, measure tangent continuity and curvature at independently supported intersections, and retain junction branches only when both fields support their geometry. Do not seed the graph with catalogue endpoints.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: Blind transfer structures can interrupt or join subsurface property boundaries where no continuous surface fault is mapped. The proposed graph tests independent geophysical geometry rather than completing a known catalogue trace. Geometric complexity alone is not evidence of heat or permeability.

Difference from reviewed work: The local implementation has per-pixel Hessian ridges, local cross-field alignment and greedy coverage, but no along-edge orientation-transport graph or cross-field junction consistency. It is not another tip-extension or mapped-stepover prior; global novelty is unverified.

Named non-fault mimic: Intersecting dikes, unfaulted intrusive boundaries, erosional drainage intersections and acquisition seams can generate stable junctions.

N4 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 4 · LOW TO MEDIUM COST

Survey-axis notch-stability veto on gridded edge evidence

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Lowest direct discovery potential; an artefact-control test, not an independent survey; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: mag_anom (owner-mirror band 1); rtp (owner-mirror band 2); tc (owner-mirror band 6); USGS published flight-line geometry metadata, no new line-level binary required for this gridded stress test

Physical signature: In identically tapered local windows of the cached grids, stress-test magnetic/radiometric edges with conservative orientation-selective spectral attenuation around the documented survey-axis directions. Fit allowable attenuation only on visible-training data and compare with rotated-axis controls. Retain only positions whose cross-field edge evidence remains stable, while measuring loss of genuine faults parallel to the axes. Do not impose one global survey-block filter.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: Reducing acquisition-line false positives could leave more emission budget for unmapped geological structures. Stability under a notch is only an artefact-sensitivity test; it cannot prove independent repeatability or establish fault presence.

Difference from reviewed work: No survey-axis spectral perturbation stability test is implemented in the local MSPTCD feature set. Unlike the older cross-flight-line proposal, this can be tested using cached grids; true independent flight/tie-line confirmation still needs external observations and is not claimed viable here.

Named non-fault mimic: Real east-west or north-south faults may be attenuated; gridding, resampling, dike swarms and anisotropic lithology can also appear directionally stable.
