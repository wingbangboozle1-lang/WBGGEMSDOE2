H1 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 1 · HIGH: DEM PROCESSING AND MULTIPLE HYDROLOGIC CONTROLS COST

Repeated, catchment-normalized cross-drainage deflections

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Highest qualitative potential in this shortlist; untested; no numerical uplift forecast; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS 3DEP bare-earth DEM, exact study tiles unverified; derived drainage network and upstream contributing area; owner-mirrored slope only as a comparison

Physical signature: Ask whether several independent streams change direction or bed gradient consistently on crossing a candidate line, normalized for drainage area and relief; compare with randomized orientations and other lithology boundaries.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A structure may leave consistent offsets across several streams even where the surface trace is interrupted; the signals are hypothesized evidence, not proof that unmapped faults must be surface-expressed.

Difference from reviewed work: Requires replicated cross-channel geometry and catchment normalization, unlike MSPTCD's multiscale Hessian scarp and flank features or a single slope/chi corridor. The exact relevant newer fleet implementations were not checked.

Named non-fault mimic: Lithological boundaries, terraces, alluvial fans, stream capture, roads and inherited drainage can create repeated deflections.

Data/access status: Official 3DEP products are free and unrestricted; exact AOI tiles, resolution and binary access have not been established. No new data or features downloaded.

External source/access: USGS 3DEP products and services ↗. Public page checked 2026-10-27: free without use restrictions. Study tiles, coverage and binary access not verified; this is not yet demonstrated obtainable in this sandbox.

H2 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 2 · HIGH: SURVEY RESOLUTION, REPROJECTION, INDEPENDENT SOURCE OVERLAP AND ANTI-LEAKAGE CHECKS COST

Multi-depth magnetotelluric conductance coherence with uncertainty

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Moderate potential; explicitly limited by depth and resolution; untested; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS Great Basin conductance maps at five depth ranges; original MT station/uncertainty metadata; potential-field edge orientation only as context

Physical signature: Compare the orientation and location of conductance transitions across separately resolved depth intervals, propagate uncertainty and covariance from the common inversion, and reject apparent continuity in undersampled areas; do not treat the intervals as independent observations.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A buried crustal electrical boundary could be associated with a concealed fault, but a deep conductor need not map to a 100 m surface trace and resolution may be far too coarse.

Difference from reviewed work: Uses externally derived multiple depth slices and resolution/uncertainty rather than MSPTCD's single conductive-surface flank comparison or gravity-magnetic dip shift; shared source data and overlap must first be established.

Named non-fault mimic: Clays, saline fluids, lithologic contacts and gridding artefacts also create conductance boundaries; a conductor can be geologically unrelated to a fault.

Data/access status: USGS describes five depth ranges from 2 to 200 km; the release is marked CC0. Exact binary access, study-area coverage and relationship to the organizer feature bands have not been verified.

External source/access: USGS Electrical Conductance Maps of the Great Basin ↗. Public release page checked 2026-10-27 and marked CC0. Actual raster download, local resolution and usable overlap remain unverified.

H3 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 3 · HIGH: RAW LINE DOWNLOADS, ACQUISITION-GEOMETRY RECONSTRUCTION AND COVERAGE CHECKS COST

Cross-flight-line repeatability veto for magnetic boundaries

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Uncertain direct uplift; primarily an artifact-control hypothesis; untested; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS GeoDAWN magnetic flight and tie-line measurements; line orientation, altitude and acquisition metadata; owner-mirrored magnetic raster for comparison

Physical signature: Compare independently observed boundary locations and orientations from crossing flight and tie lines; downweight structures that shift with acquisition direction or clearance.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A reproducible buried property contrast may support a candidate unseen structure; the test is an artifact veto and cannot establish that a stable contrast is a fault.

Difference from reviewed work: Tests raw acquisition repeatability, rather than MSPTCD's raster-only gradients and multiscale transforms. Crossing coverage and true acquisition independence are unverified.

Named non-fault mimic: Dikes and unfaulted lithologic contacts are stable across flight directions; sparse crossings or variable clearance can generate false disagreement.

Data/access status: The official release describes flight lines and grids and is marked CC0. Direct binary host is outside this sandbox's allowlist; usable crossing coverage and file access were not verified.

External source/access: USGS GeoDAWN airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys ↗. Public CC0 release page checked 2026-10-27. Binary download, coverage and crossing geometry remain unverified.

H4 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 4 · HIGH: INDEPENDENT ACQUISITION, ATMOSPHERIC CORRECTION, TEMPORAL CONTROL AND GEOREFERENCING COST

Repeat-pass deformation discontinuity with a pumping control

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Lower and highly uncertain potential; untested, including study-area data availability; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS InSAR deformation observations, specific target-area product not identified; multi-epoch coherence and uncertainty; available groundwater pumping or recharge context

Physical signature: Look for persistent, independently repeated displacement gradients across a candidate trace while controlling for pumping, atmospheric artefacts, phase unwrapping and seasonal changes.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: If a hydraulically or tectonically active concealed boundary produces a repeatable displacement contrast, it may reveal structures outside mapped traces; many faults are inactive and invisible to InSAR.

Difference from reviewed work: Uses multi-epoch observed deformation and a human-activity control, not MSPTCD's static geodetic and seismic raster features. No relevant-area product has been verified.

Named non-fault mimic: Groundwater pumping, recharge, atmospheric delay, landslides, mines, phase-unwrapping errors and radar decorrelation.

Data/access status: USGS describes downloadable InSAR observations for Las Vegas Valley, not necessarily the competition area. This is a methodological example, not proof of AOI coverage, obtainable binaries, usable temporal alignment or the reuse rights of underlying imagery.

External source/access: USGS Las Vegas Valley InSAR example ↗. Public USGS publication page checked 2026-10-27 and describes downloadable GIS products. Their overlap with the competition area, binary access and dataset-specific reuse rights have not been established; do not run until these are verified.
