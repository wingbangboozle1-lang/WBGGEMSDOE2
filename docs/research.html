H1 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 1 · MEDIUM–HIGH COST

Cross-fault marker displacement by paired patch registration

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Highest qualitative potential of this shortlist; untested; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: tc total radiometric counts (owner-mirror band 6; not magnetic tilt); det_elev (owner-mirror band 12); rtp (owner-mirror band 2); iso_grav_anom (owner-mirror band 13)

Physical signature: Along candidate gravity/magnetic boundaries, register radiometric and detrended-terrain texture patches on opposite sides. Compare a shared nonzero along-strike translation with a zero-displacement null using held-out texture pixels; require repeated marker offsets across independent patches, not merely two parallel edges.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: An eroded or concealed strike-slip component may displace inherited surface markers without producing a continuous mapped scarp. Coherent cross-boundary displacement is a kinematic hypothesis for an unmapped segment; it is not a fault label and may fail beneath thick cover.

Difference from reviewed work: MSPTCD uses depth-shifted edge-amplitude concordance and conductive-flank asymmetry. This proposal instead estimates displacement of repeated marker texture across a boundary, compares against a zero-offset null, and demands spatial replication. No patch-registration displacement estimator was found in the reviewed local Python source.

Named non-fault mimic: Drainage capture, terrace migration, lithological texture changes, and georegistration offsets between surveys can create apparent marker displacement.

Data/access status: Raw 19-band owner-mirrored inputs were restored and SHA-256 verified in this review, with 19 raw cached bands. They are not organizer-authenticated; the shared derived feature bank and a compliant release path are still missing. No new external dataset is required, but this proposal is blocked by lane/registry gates.

H2 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 2 · HIGH COST

Basin-margin gravity-step model versus intrusive-body null

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Moderate qualitative potential; overlaps a previously explored family; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: iso_grav_anom (owner-mirror band 13); iso_grav_anom_hg (owner-mirror band 18); rtp (owner-mirror band 2); det_elev (owner-mirror band 12)

Physical signature: Fit short cross-boundary gravity profiles to competing forward-response families: an asymmetric buried density step and a compact symmetric intrusive lens, each with the same nuisance trend and penalized parameter count. Rank held-out profile likelihood improvement of the step model, requiring stable parameters along strike and at multiple window sizes; use magnetic response as a consistency check, not a fault label.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A buried basin-bounding fault can juxtapose density units without a visible trace. Explicitly preferring a persistent asymmetric step over a compact-body null could reject strong non-fault magnetic anomalies missed by simple edge ranking.

Difference from reviewed work: Existing code calculates derivative amplitude, tilt, Hessian ridges and orientation concordance. It does not fit competing gravity forward-model families on cross-boundary profiles with held-out residuals. This is physical model discrimination rather than another edge-fusion score. Nonunique gravity inversion remains a major limitation.

Named non-fault mimic: An unfaulted depositional basin edge or dipping lithological contact can fit a density step; regional trend removal and shallow topographic mass can mimic asymmetry.

Data/access status: Raw 19-band owner-mirrored inputs were restored and SHA-256 verified in this review, with 19 raw cached bands. They are not organizer-authenticated; the shared derived feature bank and a compliant release path are still missing. No new external dataset is required, but this proposal is blocked by lane/registry gates.

H3 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 3 · HIGH COST

Repeated cross-drainage deflection across a candidate structure

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Lower qualitative potential; external-data feasibility unverified; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS 3DEP bare-earth digital elevation model (specific tiles and resolution not established here); det_elev_slope (owner-mirror band 19) for comparison only

Physical signature: Derive channels from absolute elevation, measure repeated channel azimuth changes, knickzones, and opposing-bank relief across the same candidate line, and normalize by upstream contributing area; require several independent crossings rather than a single scarp.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A buried or eroded fault may deflect multiple channels without leaving a continuous scarp, creating a geomorphic expression beyond catalogue segment endpoints. Channel behaviour is supporting evidence, not a fault label.

Difference from reviewed work: Different from single-scarp, slope-asymmetry, chi-corridor and dotted-ridge methods because it tests repeated cross-channel geometry using an absolute DEM and catchment normalization. Fleet-wide novelty is unverified.

Named non-fault mimic: Lithologic contacts, differential erosion, alluvial fans, inherited drainage, capture, roads, and terraces can deflect channels.

Data/access status: Not viable in this checkout until the exact official DEM tiles, coverage, resolution and permitted download are verified; none were downloaded.

External source/access: USGS 3DEP products and services ↗. The cited USGS page is recorded in the source register as describing free, unrestricted 3DEP products. Study-area tile coverage and actual binary access have not been checked; therefore the data are not yet verified obtainable for this experiment. Current sandbox allowlist excludes this host; exact binary obtainability remains unverified and the proposal is not viable here.

H4 · QUALITATIVE PRIOR RANK 4 · HIGH COST

Cross-flight-line repeatability of buried magnetic edges

Expected spatially blocked HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Lowest direct prediction potential; primarily an artefact-control experiment; not a leaderboard score estimate.

Layers: USGS GeoDAWN magnetic flight-line observations and acquisition-direction metadata; mag_anom (owner-mirror band 1) and tmi_hg (owner-mirror band 3) for gridded comparison

Physical signature: Estimate edge normals independently from crossing flight-line directions and retain only anomalies whose location and orientation are reproducible across acquisition directions; use disagreement as an artefact veto, not as a positive fault label.

Why it could target a catalogue-missing fault: A real subsurface physical-property boundary potentially associated with an unmapped fault should be spatially stable across independent acquisition directions, unlike some flight-line artefacts. This test improves measurement reliability; it does not establish that a stable edge is a fault.

Difference from reviewed work: This is an acquisition-geometry robustness test, not another point-density, terrain-scarp, geothermal-proximity, or catalogue-gap score. No local implementation is present; fleet-wide novelty is unverified.

Named non-fault mimic: A lithologic contact, dike, or non-fault magnetic body is also stable across flight directions; uneven line coverage can create false disagreement.

Data/access status: The USGS release page is recorded as listing flight-line data and grids, but the line-level files and usable crossing coverage were not downloaded or verified in this environment. Not viable here until that check succeeds.

External source/access: USGS GeoDAWN airborne magnetic and radiometric survey release ↗. The public release page is recorded as available and CC0 in the source register; its binary source host was not reachable in the prior sandbox check. Exact flight-line file access and spatial overlap remain unverified. Current sandbox allowlist excludes this host; exact binary obtainability remains unverified and the proposal is not viable here.
