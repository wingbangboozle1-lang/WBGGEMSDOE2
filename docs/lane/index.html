Why 0.2778 topped these sites

  • With α = 0.2 and β = 0.8, one unit of hit mass is worth up to 17 units of false-alarm mass at s = 0.2778 — long, thin and correct beats wide.
  • Every file in the 0.24–0.28 band is 37.6k–42.3k binary cells whose 300 m halo covers 0.151–0.167 of the footprint: one saturated design, re-parameterised.
  • GEMSDOE32 …h33-2-b2… is the GEMSDOE25/30 array minus exactly 6,436 dots, 100 % of them within 2 px of the mapped catalogue. The 0.0178 gain is a pruning step, consistent with the organizer ruling that known-fault pixels are masked.

Why 0.2778 scored highest — the inverted formula

With DTI = T/(T + 0.2F + 0.8(N−T)), and the one pair of fleet files that is provably the same detector at two masses (44,090 dots → 0.2600, its 37,654-dot pruned subset → 0.2778), the pair solves to N = 12,783 scored truth pixels and 40.9 % coverage for both. The implied coverage is stable (0.407–0.410) even when the reported scores move by ±0.001 or 10 % of the mass is exempt from the false-positive term; the truth size is not (11.4k–12.9k).

Can we beat 0.2778?

At ≈38,000 unit cells the published formula requires covering 35–46 % of the hidden expert labels to reach 0.2778, and 50–78 % to reach the live leaderboard top of 0.4544. This checkout contains no detector that covers that share of unseen faults: the best per-pixel model reached AUC 0.57 inside a region, and the external lidar-scarp and radiometric products 0.569 and 0.549. So: a unique, well-formed, in-envelope submission — yes; a guaranteed improvement — no.
