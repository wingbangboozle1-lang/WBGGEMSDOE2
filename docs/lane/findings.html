0. The question

Why did h33-h33-2-b2-20261004T220000Z-e5eb6e7e-zeros (GEMSDOE32) reach 0.2778 — the highest score among the nine focus sites — and can we generate a submission that scores higher?

Short answer. 0.2778 is not a geological discovery; it is the best point yet found inside a narrow, saturated plateau that every dot-based submission in this fleet occupies. The plateau is set by the metric's own economics (alpha 0.2 / beta 0.8 make one unit of missed truth four times more expensive than one unit of false alarm), and 0.2778 was reached by deleting 6,436 dots that sat within 2 px of the already-mapped catalogue — a pruning step, not a new detection. To beat it you must cover a larger share of the hidden expert labels at the same mass; nothing measured in this checkout shows that any of the fleet's methods does that, and the leaderboard's current best (0.4544) is 0.177 above this plateau. We can build a unique, well-formed, envelope-matched submission — and we do — but claiming it will exceed 0.2778 would not be supported by any measurement available here.

2. The metric sets the plateau — the algebra

Write T = weighted true-positive mass, F = weighted false-positive mass, N = number of hidden truth pixels. Then FN = N - T and

DTI = T / ((1-beta) T + alpha F + beta N)

Differentiating (src/gemsdoe/fastdti.py::marginal_rule):

improvement <=> (1 - (1-beta) s) dT > alpha s dF

At the observed plateau value s = 0.2778 this gives 0.9444 dT > 0.0556 dF, i.e.

one unit of true-positive mass is worth up to 17 units of false-positive mass.

Three consequences, all MEASURED or exact arithmetic:

  1. Width is expensive, length is cheap. A 300 m kernel already tolerates 3 px of misregistration, so painting a 5-px band along a trace pays 0.2 x 5 per 5 cells of length for the same truth mass a 1-px line would collect.
  2. Mass is a budget. Beyond the point where extra cells stop touching new truth, every added cell strictly lowers DTI. At 38,000 unit cells this candidate's FP term is 7,600 against a beta N term of 8,000-32,000 (N = 10k-40k) — the emission is a material part of the denominator, not a rounding error.
  3. The known map is free. Under the staff ruling, emission on known USGS/INGENIOUS pixels costs nothing. The corollary is sharper: emission just outside that mask costs full price. B2's 0.0178 improvement over its parent is the measured value of acting on that corollary ($6,436 dots x 0.2 = 1,287 of FP mass removed).

Inverting the formula: how big is the hidden truth, and what did B2 cover?

DTI = T / (T + 0.2 F + 0.8 (N - T)). Given a reported score s and a measured mass M, and taking the conservative F = M (every dot pays full price), the implied kernel-weighted coverage is T = s (0.2M + 0.8N) / (1 - 0.2s). One score therefore pins T once N is known — and N is pinned by the single pair of files that is provably the same detector with different mass: GEMSDOE25/30 D2.8 (44,090 dots, 0.2600) and GEMSDOE32 B2 (37,654 dots, 0.2778), where B2 is D2.8 minus 6,436 collar dots and pruning cannot change T:

0.2778 (0.2 x 37,654 + 0.8 N) / (1 - 0.0556) = 0.2600 (0.2 x 44,090 + 0.8 N) / (1 - 0.0520) => N = 12,783 scored truth pixels => T = 5,223 kernel-weighted truth mass for both files => B2 covered 40.9 % of the hidden expert labels

scripts/gemsdoe_metric_model.py, output data_public/gemsdoe_metric_model.json. The number is a model estimate, not a measurement: it assumes the reported scores are accurate, that F = M, and that the 6,436 removed collar dots carried no scored truth. It is falsifiable — see the predictions below. Sensitivity is recomputed in the JSON: moving both reported scores by +-0.001, or letting only 90 % of the mass pay full price, moves N between 11,432 and 12,864 while the implied B2 coverage stays at 0.407-0.410. The coverage estimate is therefore robust even though the truth size is not.

Falsifiable prediction 1 — mass is pure cost at fixed coverage. Holding B2's coverage and re-emitting at other masses predicts:

mass 10,000 20,000 30,000 37,654 (B2) 44,090 (D2.8) 60,000 90,000
predicted DTI 0.394 0.342 0.302 0.2778 0.2600 0.224 0.178

The 44,090 entry reproduces the independent D2.8 report (0.2600) exactly, which is the one out-of-sample check the pair can supply.

Falsifiable prediction 2 — files that spread mass without covering truth collapse. Inverting all 92 fleet rasters that carry a reported score gives a median implied coverage of 21.1 % (10th-90th percentile 3.6 %-45.3 %). The extremes behave exactly as the formula demands: gems79-tilt-ring-100000dots (100,000 dots, 0.0005) implies coverage 0.001 — a hundred thousand dots that claim almost nothing and pay for everything; gems22-h23-a-dti-optimal-emission-6pct (335,879 dots, 0.1002) implies coverage 0.619 — most of the truth, nearly cancelled by its own false-positive mass.

Falsifiable prediction 3 — the ceiling needs coverage, not tuning. With N = 12,783 and F = M, reaching 0.4544 requires f >= 0.50 at 40,000 cells (or f >= 0.44 at 20,000). The nine focus sites span f = 0.40-0.48 (best: GEMSDOE31 add-arm-d28, 55,304 cells, 0.2708, f = 0.477); the 0.24-0.28 band is therefore a coverage plateau, and moving past it needs a different class of detector, not a different parameterisation of this one.

What this means for the submitted file. At 38,000 cells the same formula maps coverage to score as 0.15 -> 0.105, 0.20 -> 0.139, 0.25 -> 0.173, 0.30 -> 0.206, 0.35 -> 0.239, 0.41 -> 0.277. The candidate is at parity with B2 only if its detector covers 41 % of the hidden labels; it beats B2 only above that. Nothing measured in this container can demonstrate 41 % on unseen faults, which is why the estimate is stated as a band and not as a promise.

3. What actually distinguishes 0.2778 from its neighbours (MEASURED)

scripts/gemsdoe_final.py, data/work/site_overlap.json and data/work/fleet_scored.json:

  • GEMSDOE25 dotted-h19-5-d2-8-... and GEMSDOE30 d28-poisson300m-offcat-44090-... decode to identical prediction arrays (44,090 dots) despite different bytes and different reported scores (0.2600 both).
  • GEMSDOE32 B2 is that array with exactly 6,436 dots removed; 100 % of the removed dots lie within 2 px of a mapped catalogue pixel, and B2 retains 0 dots within 2 px (2,171 within 3 px).
  • Re-measured on the GeoTIFFs this session: D2.8 has 44,090 dots whose 300 m halo covers 15.68 % of the in-footprint cells (B2 after pruning: 13.69 %), 19.5 % of dots sit within 3 px of a mapped fault, the median dot is 15 px (1.5 km) from the catalogue and the 90th percentile is 64 px. This is not a catalogue-hugging field; it is a sparse regional field that avoids the masked collar.
  • Everything else in the 0.24-0.28 band is the same object: 37.6k-61.3k binary dots, 300 m halo covering 13.7-15.7 % of the footprint, median 15-20 px from the catalogue.

The band is therefore one design repeated with small parameter changes. Its ceiling is a property of the design, not of the geology it encodes.

4. Experiments run this session (3, as budgeted)

E1 — supervised pixel detector on the 19 organizer bands (negative)

scripts/gemsdoe_train.py, HistGradientBoosting, 76 features (per band: robust z-score, 300 m gradient magnitude, 300 m Laplacian-of-Gaussian, 600 m smoothed), 4 spatially blocked whole-segment folds, 2-px halo excluded from the negative pool.

  • Leakage canary fired first: with distance-to-visible-catalogue included, the surface collapsed — p = 1 exactly on visible faults, floor probability everywhere else, single-feature AUC 1.000. Fixed by removing the layer (scripts/gemsdoe_train.py docstring records it).
  • After the fix: fold-0 AUC 0.4809 against withheld segments (15,122 truth pixels, 400k background) — worse than random. Best DTI over threshold sweep 0.0139 at 704,530 cells; every smaller-mass setting was lower.
  • Diagnostic of why: single-feature AUCs computed inside the fold's own blocks (which removes regional confounding) top out at 0.5745 (det_elev_slope gradient magnitude); median |AUC − 0.5| over 76 layers is 0.0225 and only 11/76 layers exceed |0.05|. The same test on the external lidar-scarp product peaks at 0.5693 (upface_max) and on GeoDAWN radiometrics at 0.5490 (K).
  • Conclusion: at 100 m, per-pixel geophysics does not localise unseen fault lines in this dataset. That is a negative result and a deliverable.

E2 — metric-aware emission from a multi-physics curvature consensus

src/gemsdoe/candidates.py: absolute Laplacian-of-Gaussian response per band, one vote per physics category (magnetic / gravity / topographic / strain / seismic), smoothed, then greedy placement by score with a minimum-separation constraint and a 2-px exclusion collar around the mapped catalogue (the B2 lesson). Separation radius is tuned so that the 300 m halo covers 0.15-0.17 of the footprint — the measured envelope of the highest-scoring observed files.

  • The repository's standing rule "do not spend a submission slot on an idea that has not beaten the current holdout best" cannot be satisfied by any file that is also good on the leaderboard — the two orderings disagree in sign at the top of the range that is actually observed. E4 — 76-layer gradient-boosting detector, out-of-fold (negative, and instructive)

scripts/gemsdoe_v2_train.py (79 features: the 76 organizer-band layers plus three fold-visible catalogue-context layers; HistGradientBoosting, 150-260 iterations) writes out-of-fold surfaces for the four hidden-segment folds.

  • With a raw distance-to-visible-fault feature the model collapses to that indicator: the fold-0 surface takes two unique values, 1.0 on 45,845 cells (the visible fault count) and 3.3e-05 elsewhere. Hidden-segment coverage 0.42 %, DTI 0.0000. The distance feature is a label restatement, not a predictor — it is dropped.
  • Without it, the fold-0 surface is continuous and scores HOLDOUT-DTI 0.0139 at 704,530 predicted cells: hidden-segment coverage 0.141 against a 0.136 reach, i.e. enrichment 1.04 — no skill. data/work/v2_eval.json.

The honest reading: within this container, no configuration of the organizer layers predicts withheld catalogue segments beyond chance. That is why the delivered file is not a machine-learning classifier: the reported 0.24-0.28 band comes from coverage geometry and a 2x enrichment that we cannot reproduce or verify locally.

8. What would actually move the needle

  1. Localise better, not tune better. The gap to 0.4544 needs f of 0.5-0.78, i.e. a detector that finds most expert-mapped new faults. The only untested lever with the right physical scale is H4 (1 m lidar scarp morphometry), which needs a machine with unrestricted network access to the 3DEP links.
  2. Line-level, not pixel-level, supervision. In-block AUC 0.57 says the pixel representation is the bottleneck; segment candidates with a curvilinear detector and classify segments.
  3. Exploit the mask deliberately. With known pixels free, the optimal frontier is a coverage-maximising placement problem under a mass budget; solve it as submodular max-cover over the unmasked cells with a kernel-radius constraint (this session's greedy is a first-order version of that).
  4. Get receipts. Three submissions per week are allowed; a single submitted file with a receipt would replace every USER-REPORTED number in this analysis.
