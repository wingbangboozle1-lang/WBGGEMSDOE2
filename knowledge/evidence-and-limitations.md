# Evidence, forensic findings, and limitations

## The central contradiction: no eligible new file

The [unmodified shared uniqueness definition](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/57GEMSDOE/blob/822a0ce5546c7b6749cdd82eb145b6b3e616603d/src/gems57/uniqueness.py) defines support as finite positive values and applies the directional fraction of a candidate's support within three pixels of a registry raster. The [GEMSDOE48 continuous witness](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE48/blob/268ec599f51c517cc3485da077c24d83af5be81d/docs/downloads/gemsdoe48-h58-continuous-pignistic-20261008T175914Z-e58.tif) is present in the [pinned shared registry inventory](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/57GEMSDOE/blob/822a0ce5546c7b6749cdd82eb145b6b3e616603d/evidence/registry_refreshed.json). Its bytes hash to `59ef8e7d8374e228bc1e41376f03fc67dd5b8a5e6804885a1ae802f399140b89` and are positive on **all 5,167,373** pixels of the hash-pinned mirrored footprint. Its grid matches that footprint.

For any nonempty candidate support inside that footprint, every candidate pixel is at distance zero from witness support. Its directional overlap is consequently **100%**, necessarily above the literal **70%** ceiling. This is a mathematical impossibility under the current definitions, not an assertion that all geology is identical. Independently, the listed [13GEMSDOE lattice mirror](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE24/blob/5cfab74597cd1d0a271e4640b4cc34511e56ca59/inputs/calibration/13gems_20261001_r13-lattice-s5_v2_nan-outside.tif) covers 99.8713% of the footprint within three pixels; at 37,654 dots its overlap must be at least 82.34% (because only 6,649 eligible cells are outside its halo).

The scope of the proof depends on the mirrored footprint and the shared positive-pixel interpretation. A proposed rule that excludes dense rasters or distinguishes continuous surfaces from dot fields **changes the requested rule** and has not been authorized. A witness is sufficient to prove failure; this is not a claim that we exhaustively compared all historical files. The [run card](../reports/run-card.json) is negative, with absent values for a nonexistent candidate—not zero-valued invented results.

## GEMSDOE32 H33: what is established and what is not

The brief attributes **0.2778 (USER-REPORTED CLAIM; no verified receipt)** to `h33-h33-2-b2`. The [original GEMSDOE32 audit at commit c39ee46](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE32/blob/c39ee46ea79812d8de7de9c037685a709ba2aa87/docs/downloads/submissions_manifest.json) instead called that same candidate **UNSCORED**, gave a projection of **0.2747**, and listed its receipt as null. The [original PR](https://github.com/buffedlizard55-lab/GEMSDOE32/pull/9) also says no organizer score existed there at that time. A later score cannot be disproved from this alone, but an exact file-to-score receipt has not been verified. A public leaderboard entry does not identify its uploaded TIFF. Therefore we cannot honestly explain a proven 0.2778 or assert that our future candidate will beat it.

Independent pixel checks, using the pinned historical rasters for research only, establish:

* GEMSDOE25's D2.8 support has 44,090 dots and is a subset of the H19-5 support. GEMSDOE30 has the **same in-footprint support**, although the files are not byte-identical (different encodings/outside values).
* GEMSDOE31 has 40,199 dots and equals D2.8 with pixels at distance ≤1 pixel from the mapped catalogue removed (zero XOR pixels).
* GEMSDOE32 H33 has 37,654 dots and equals D2.8 with pixels at distance ≤2 pixels removed (zero XOR pixels). Removing the 2,545 additional near-catalogue dots is a real raster change—not evidence of a causal leaderboard gain.

[The official metric](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) weights false positives by α=0.2 and false negatives by β=0.8 with a 300 m linear kernel. Distributed predictions can recover multiple nearby truth pixels but can also incur false-positive penalties and miss genuine corrections. [DrivenData staff](https://community.drivendata.org/t/scoring-clarification-are-known-usgs-ingenious-faults-masked-when-scoring-and-are-they-in-the-final-round-label-set/11516/4) says only the exact known-fault pixels are masked, nearby pixels are scored normally, and new truth may occur near known traces. A collar is therefore a hypothesis, not a universally optimal correction. Hidden evaluation labels and a file-linked score receipt are unavailable, so neither the cause of the alleged score nor improvement over it is verified.

The nine focus-site analyses compare **directional three-pixel support overlap** against each other, not just SHA-256. Each exceeds 70% against another focus raster, so none is unique under the stated rule. They are historical research artifacts, never a new recommended upload. The table is in [the executive summary](../executive-summary.html); exact pins, pairwise overlaps, and hashes are in [focus-review.json](../reports/focus-review.json).

## Limitations and irregularities

1. **Data provenance:** the 19-band, 418,912,844-byte feature stack, labels and grid were restored from the pinned public owner bridge using the shared 57GEMSDOE script; SHA-256 matches the bridge receipts, not an independent authenticated DrivenData download. The mirrored sample has 0/1 values, although the public problem description calls the official sample a fault-absence example. Treat the mirror's origin and sample semantics as unconfirmed. Band descriptions are copied from the mirror, not independently verified; notably its `depth_to_base_surf` description says basement, while the official problem describes depth to a conductive base. The `tc` and earthquake abbreviations are also ambiguous until checked against authoritative metadata. [Data receipt](../reports/data-preparation-20261026.json) and [band cache receipt](../reports/feature-cache-20261026.json).
2. **Range error:** official instructions require a float32 single band, values in [0,1], EPSG:32611, identical shape/transform, and null/NaN outside the bounds. The public wording says “outside the bounds” and does not clearly resolve how uncovered cells *inside* the rectangular grid should be represented. The shared writer emits all-finite zeros outside the mirrored footprint; whether the portal accepts that convention, or outside NaNs without the reported range error, is **not independently established here**. No local format pass is an organizer acceptance. `scripts/validate_raster.py` uses the shared on-disk validator and always denies upload without the missing gates; inspecting the historical H33 bytes passed its conservative format check but still overlapped the universal witness at 100%.
3. **Evaluation:** no new surface, leakage canary, holdout, 95% interval, TIF, name or note was produced after the pre-placement stop. There is no candidate score estimate. The private labels and submission-page receipts are inaccessible. A mapped-fault hide-and-recover proxy is also subject to sampling/visibility shift and cannot be equated to the hidden-fault leaderboard.
4. **Rules and quota:** the [PDF](https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy26osti/96647.pdf) requires disclosure of generative AI use and reproducible solution assets; consult eligibility and licensing. Its three-per-week language is clarified by [DrivenData staff](https://community.drivendata.org/t/submission-amount-question/11553/2): the competition website limit prevails. The current account-specific available quota is unknown.
5. **Network/hardware:** this sandbox cannot access DrivenData or official USGS/GDR binary hosts directly; web retrieval can read public pages. The competition data tab redirects to login. No GPU was observed (`nvidia-smi` unavailable). The feature cache is ignored and ephemeral to keep large data out of Git; rehydrate with `scripts/prepare_data.sh`.
6. **Current feed:** the displayed public leaderboard is a timestamped observation, not a file-specific receipt; the scheduled Pages refresh preserves a prior observation and explicitly marks failed/stale retrievals. It cannot authenticate account-specific scores, inspect private test data, or validate a slot.
