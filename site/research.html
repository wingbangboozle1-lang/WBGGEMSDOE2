H1 · QUALITATIVE POTENTIAL RANK 1 · HIGH: DEM PROCESSING AND MULTIPLE HYDROLOGIC CONTROLS COST

Repeated, catchment-normalized cross-drainage deflections

Expected HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Highest qualitative potential in this shortlist; untested; no numerical uplift forecast; this is not a score estimate.

Layers: USGS 3DEP bare-earth DEM, exact study tiles unverified; derived drainage network and upstream contributing area; owner-mirrored slope only as a comparison.

Physical signature: Ask whether several independent streams change direction or bed gradient consistently on crossing a candidate line, normalized for drainage area and relief; compare with randomized orientations and other lithology boundaries.

Why it may target an uncatalogued fault: A structure may leave consistent offsets across several streams even where the surface trace is interrupted; the signals are hypothesized evidence, not proof that unmapped faults must be surface-expressed.

Difference from reviewed methods: Requires replicated cross-channel geometry and catchment normalization, unlike MSPTCD's multiscale Hessian scarp and flank features or a single slope/chi corridor. The exact relevant newer fleet implementations were not checked.

Named non-fault mimic: Lithological boundaries, terraces, alluvial fans, stream capture, roads and inherited drainage can create repeated deflections.

Data/access status: Official 3DEP products are free and unrestricted; exact AOI tiles, resolution and binary access have not been established. No new data or features downloaded.

Official source and access check: USGS 3DEP products and services ↗. Public page checked 2026-10-27: free without use restrictions. Study tiles, coverage and binary access not verified; this is not yet demonstrated obtainable in this sandbox.

H2 · QUALITATIVE POTENTIAL RANK 2 · HIGH: SURVEY RESOLUTION, REPROJECTION, INDEPENDENT SOURCE OVERLAP AND ANTI-LEAKAGE CHECKS COST

Multi-depth magnetotelluric conductance coherence with uncertainty

Expected HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Moderate potential; explicitly limited by depth and resolution; untested; this is not a score estimate.

Layers: USGS Great Basin conductance maps at five depth ranges; original MT station/uncertainty metadata; potential-field edge orientation only as context.

Physical signature: Compare the orientation and location of conductance transitions across separately resolved depth intervals, propagate uncertainty and covariance from the common inversion, and reject apparent continuity in undersampled areas; do not treat the intervals as independent observations.

Why it may target an uncatalogued fault: A buried crustal electrical boundary could be associated with a concealed fault, but a deep conductor need not map to a 100 m surface trace and resolution may be far too coarse.

Difference from reviewed methods: Uses externally derived multiple depth slices and resolution/uncertainty rather than MSPTCD's single conductive-surface flank comparison or gravity-magnetic dip shift; shared source data and overlap must first be established.

Named non-fault mimic: Clays, saline fluids, lithologic contacts and gridding artefacts also create conductance boundaries; a conductor can be geologically unrelated to a fault.

Data/access status: USGS describes five depth ranges from 2 to 200 km; the release is marked CC0. Exact binary access, study-area coverage and relationship to the organizer feature bands have not been verified.

Official source and access check: USGS Electrical Conductance Maps of the Great Basin ↗. Public release page checked 2026-10-27 and marked CC0. Actual raster download, local resolution and usable overlap remain unverified.

H3 · QUALITATIVE POTENTIAL RANK 3 · HIGH: RAW LINE DOWNLOADS, ACQUISITION-GEOMETRY RECONSTRUCTION AND COVERAGE CHECKS COST

Cross-flight-line repeatability veto for magnetic boundaries

Expected HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Uncertain direct uplift; primarily an artifact-control hypothesis; untested; this is not a score estimate.

Layers: USGS GeoDAWN magnetic flight and tie-line measurements; line orientation, altitude and acquisition metadata; owner-mirrored magnetic raster for comparison.

Physical signature: Compare independently observed boundary locations and orientations from crossing flight and tie lines; downweight structures that shift with acquisition direction or clearance.

Why it may target an uncatalogued fault: A reproducible buried property contrast may support a candidate unseen structure; the test is an artifact veto and cannot establish that a stable contrast is a fault.

Difference from reviewed methods: Tests raw acquisition repeatability, rather than MSPTCD's raster-only gradients and multiscale transforms. Crossing coverage and true acquisition independence are unverified.

Named non-fault mimic: Dikes and unfaulted lithologic contacts are stable across flight directions; sparse crossings or variable clearance can generate false disagreement.

Data/access status: The official release describes flight lines and grids and is marked CC0. Direct binary host is outside this sandbox's allowlist; usable crossing coverage and file access were not verified.

Official source and access check: USGS GeoDAWN airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys ↗. Public CC0 release page checked 2026-10-27. Binary download, coverage and crossing geometry remain unverified.

H4 · QUALITATIVE POTENTIAL RANK 4 · HIGH: INDEPENDENT ACQUISITION, ATMOSPHERIC CORRECTION, TEMPORAL CONTROL AND GEOREFERENCING COST

Repeat-pass deformation discontinuity with a pumping control

Expected HOLDOUT-DTI potential: Lower and highly uncertain potential; untested, including study-area data availability; this is not a score estimate.

Layers: USGS InSAR deformation observations, specific target-area product not identified; multi-epoch coherence and uncertainty; available groundwater pumping or recharge context.

Physical signature: Look for persistent, independently repeated displacement gradients across a candidate trace while controlling for pumping, atmospheric artefacts, phase unwrapping and seasonal changes.

Why it may target an uncatalogued fault: If a hydraulically or tectonically active concealed boundary produces a repeatable displacement contrast, it may reveal structures outside mapped traces; many faults are inactive and invisible to InSAR.

Difference from reviewed methods: Uses multi-epoch observed deformation and a human-activity control, not MSPTCD's static geodetic and seismic raster features. No relevant-area product has been verified.

Named non-fault mimic: Groundwater pumping, recharge, atmospheric delay, landslides, mines, phase-unwrapping errors and radar decorrelation.

Data/access status: USGS describes downloadable InSAR observations for Las Vegas Valley, not necessarily the competition area. This is a methodological example, not proof of AOI coverage, obtainable binaries, usable temporal alignment or the reuse rights of underlying imagery.

Official source and access check: USGS Las Vegas Valley InSAR example ↗. Public USGS publication page checked 2026-10-27 and describes downloadable GIS products. Their overlap with the competition area, binary access and dataset-specific reuse rights have not been established; do not run until these are verified.

Proposal only · not tested

GEMSDOE32 B2: measured historical raster lineage

On the hash-checked owner mirrors, the GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 D2.8 masks are identical. The GEMSDOE32 B2 mask is the D2.8 mask after removing dots within two pixels of the mirrored catalogue: 44,090 to 37,654 positive pixels. GEMSDOE36 adds 1,200 dots to B2 and removes none. These are REGISTRY-MEASUREMENTS on owner-mirrored bytes—not an organizer receipt, causal explanation, or new-score estimate. See the full registry audit ↗ and B2 forensic analysis ↗.

Validation before any slot

  1. Resolve the continuous-prior interpretation and restore a complete, fresh, immutable registry; do not move predictions to exploit footprint edges.
  2. Record the explicit assigned method lane. This shortlist does not assign one.
  3. Restore and authenticate inputs and the pinned shared feature stack/evaluator/writer. Owner-mirrored raw bands were reverified in an ignored session cache, which may not persist; the derived shared feature bank remains absent and mirror hashes do not establish organizer provenance.
  4. On whole-segment spatial holdouts with a buffer, derive catalogue features from visible faults only, mask visible faults pixel-exactly, and run each feature-alone leakage canary. AUC above 0.90 or its inverse is an alarm.
  5. Use the shared pooled DTI evaluator (alpha 0.2, beta 0.8, 300 m triangular kernel); compare against a matched same-fold incumbent and report evaluator version, withheld positives, and a spatial-block 95% CI.
  6. Stop after three experiments or two hours. HOLDOUT-DTI is not a leaderboard score; promotion to a real slot is a separate selector step under the current quota.
