The uniqueness stop

The 13GEMSDOE stride-5 lattice is in the recorded registry. In the mirrored footprint, 5,160,724 of 5,167,373 cells lie within three pixels of one of its dots. At a budget of 37,654 distinct dots, even if every one of the remaining 6,649 uncovered cells were used, at least 31,005 dots would overlap: 82.34%. The strict limit is 70%. This is a counting bound, not a geological test.

A budget of at most 22,163 dots is not ruled out by this particular counting argument. A second registered continuous prior is positive throughout the footprint; under the shared literal finite-positive support definition it gives every nonempty candidate 100% overlap. Whether a continuous field should count as dots is unresolved, not silently exempted. Of the 6,649 uncovered cells, 6,485 are within three pixels of a footprint boundary or hole; selecting them merely to defeat the rule would be an acquisition artifact, not geological discovery. A partial inventory can prove a failure, never a global pass.

At review time, GEMSDOE27's site called H31 approved, but our exact-file check measured 99.61% of its dots within three pixels of the lattice. That site's smaller inventory and this prompt's literal rule are different. GEMSDOE32 H47 similarly overlaps the lattice by 99.79%. Neither is approved here. View the byte-bound audit.

A parallel TIFF has 99.38% of its dots within three pixels of the lattice and marks 5,132,809 footprint cells NoData. It is not eligible for submission. The parallel nine-file-only novelty claim was not an any-registry check. A second, synthetic conduit TIFF has 99.8726% lattice overlap at its own threshold and no holdout. Neither is eligible. Read the independent recheck.

The B2 lineage

The audited GEMSDOE25 and GEMSDOE30 prediction masks are identical inside the footprint. GEMSDOE32 B2 is exactly the first mask with dots at most two pixels from the mirrored catalogue removed. The GEMSDOE36 mask adds 1,200 dots and removes none from B2. These are observable raster relationships. They do not authenticate the owner-reported score, reveal hidden truth, or prove causation.

The official distance-weighted Tversky metric gives nearby dots partial truth credit. Sparse, well-positioned predictions may retain coverage while reducing false-positive mass. The public/private test sets can still differ. DrivenData staff states that known-fault pixels are masked, not that a surrounding buffer is excluded.

Four research hypotheses

Ranked by judgment about potential signal and cost, not a score forecast. The repository was empty at checkout; exact novelty across all parallel projects is not established. No hypothesis in this review lane was executed after the feasibility stop; a parallel session reported three separate experiments.

01 / PROVISIONAL LANE · MEDIUM COST

Depth-shifted gravity–magnetic discontinuity

Use mirrored isostatic gravity, reduced-to-pole magnetics, depth to conductive base and detrended elevation. Compare multi-scale signed gradients after a bounded cross-normal shift based on conductive-base depth and a dip proxy; whether that depth tracks basement is unverified. A buried offset could continue beneath basin fill beyond a mapped trace. Mimics: unfaulted lithologic contacts, dikes and survey striping. This differs as proposed from the reviewed pseudo-gravity-residual and simple fusion approaches; a wider novelty check is still needed.

02 / MEDIUM COST

Conductive flank asymmetry

Compare conductivity on opposite sides of a gravity/depth step against local background. A conductive flank might accompany a concealed fluid pathway. Mimics: clay, saline groundwater and sedimentary contacts. This is a side-to-side test rather than conductivity alone; the broader fusion family has been studied.

03 / HIGH COST · SOURCE NOT YET DOWNLOADED

Catchment-normalized drainage deflection

Use a 3DEP absolute-elevation DEM to compare repeated cross-channel deflections and opposing-bank relief. A buried fault could disturb multiple drainages. Mimics: lithologic erosion, inherited channels, roads and terraces. USGS describes 3DEP data as free, but relevant tiles and coverage have not been verified here.

04 / HIGH COST · SOURCE NOT YET DOWNLOADED

Flight-line repeatability veto

Independently grid GeoDAWN flight and tie-line magnetic data, then retain edges that repeat across acquisition directions. This targets survey artifacts rather than simply adding another lineament. Mimics: real dikes and contacts; uneven survey geometry may also create false disagreement. The USGS release lists line CSVs; the needed files and overlap could not be downloaded in this sandbox.

Research grounding: the USGS Argenta Rise study combines gravity, magnetics and MT for a possible blind system; it does not validate our transform. The USGS play-fairway synthesis describes multi-observation exploration. The GeoDAWN release notes variable survey clearance. These sources support the question, not a numerical prediction.
